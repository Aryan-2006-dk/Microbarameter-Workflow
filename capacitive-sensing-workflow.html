From a pneumatic breath to a differential capacitance reading.

Ambient air enters through a pneumatic opening and slowly charges a sealed reference volume. The lag between fast outside pressure and the damped reference pressure flexes the sensing membrane — and that tiny motion becomes an electrical signal.

Air Reference pressure Membrane motion Capacitance CIN+ CIN− EXC
Start

Pneumatic Opening

Ambient air enters the sensor through a small pneumatic restrictor — the only path between outside pressure and the device.

Carries: ambient air, live pressure

Stage 1

Reference Volume

Air fills a sealed reference chamber through a narrow bleed path, so its internal pressure changes only very slowly — a damped copy of the outside pressure.

Carries: slow, damped reference pressure

Stage 2

Capacitive Membrane Deflects

Because ambient pressure moves faster than the reference volume, a tiny differential pressure appears across the thin capacitive membrane, flexing it up or down.

Carries: mechanical displacement

Stage 3

Differential Capacitance (C1\u2013C4)

The membrane's motion changes the gap to four surrounding electrode plates — C1, C2, C3 and C4 — shifting each plate's capacitance in proportion to displacement.

Carries: differential capacitance

Stage 4

Readout: CIN+ / CIN− / EXC

An excitation signal drives the plates through EXC, and the resulting differential charge is picked up on CIN+ and CIN− — the raw signal that downstream electronics convert into a pressure reading.

Carries: electrical output signal
