From raw capacitance logs to a pressure-time curve.

Every reading starts with calibration. From there, hours of logged data move through hex storage, a CSV conversion, and a summation step that turns capacitance into pressure — ending in a plotted curve.

Start

Calibration

Known reference pressures are applied to the sensor to establish its Pressure–Capacitance (P vs C) relationship — the reference curve every later reading will be measured against.

Produces: P vs C calibration curve

Stage 1

Long-Duration Data Logging

The sensor runs for an extended period under varying pressure conditions, continuously recording capacitance against time.

Carries: capacitance vs. time, raw stream

Stage 2

Raw Storage — TXT (Hex)

Each reading is written to a .txt log file, with capacitance and time stored in hexadecimal — the sensor's native output format.

Carries: hexadecimal-encoded readings

Stage 3

Conversion to CSV (Decimal)

The hex log is converted into a CSV file with plain decimal values — turning the raw output into numbers that are easy to read, share, and process.

Carries: decimal capacitance & time, tabular

Stage 4

Pressure–Time Relation → Graphical Analysis

The computed pressure values, paired with their timestamps, are plotted to reveal exactly how pressure changed across the whole test.

Result: pressure vs. time curve

Stage 5

Pressure–Frequency Relation → Infrasound Detection

We obtained the frequency spectrum by FFT of the pressure-time graph and filtered for frequencies below 20Hz. Further processing done to detect infrasound

Result: Infrasound Detected
